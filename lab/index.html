Lab.

Five live models from the papers. Each model runs in the browser, with the code that draws the paper backgrounds. Hold a parameter with the slider, or let the sweep run. With reduced motion the models show a fixed frame, and the slider still works.

01

Lift against incidence

A wing section moves through a range of incidence. The comb is the load along the chord, and the arrow is the resultant. The lift comes from the Kirchhoff model, so the flow leaves the section after about 14 degrees. In the inset, the curve bends away from the straight line, and that bend is the nonlinearity the Pazy wing papers model.

From the papers Physics-Informed Data-Driven Modelling of Nonlinear Aerodynamic Forces of the Pazy Wing and Data-Driven Parametric Aeroelastic Modeling of the Pazy Wing.

02

Pitch, plunge and the wake

The section pitches and plunges near flutter, and it sheds a street of vortices. The circulation follows Theodorsen, so the wake makes the lift smaller and late. The loop on the right is the pitch-plunge orbit, and its area is the work the flow does in one cycle. Set the amplitude and see the wake and the loop change.

From the paper Data-Driven Parametric Aeroelastic Modeling of the Pazy Wing.

03

A wing tip on a flared hinge

The hinge is free: the flow turns the tip, and the tip finds its own angle. Because the axis is swept back, a fold takes incidence off the tip, which takes lift off it. Set the flare and see the fold get smaller. Below about 10 degrees nothing holds the tip, and it goes to its stop.

From the paper Absolute Nodal Coordinate Formulation for Nonlinear Multibody Modeling of Flared Hinged Wings.

04

Energy between the modes of a beam

A clamped beam moves in its first three modes, and energy moves between them. A higher mode holds the same energy in a much smaller motion, so the drawing scales to fit while the bars keep the true energies. The dashed envelope is the largest deflection the energies permit. Move the energy toward one mode.

From the paper Capturing & Bounding Nonlinear Modal Energy Transfer for Geometrically Exact Beams using Semidefinite Programming.

05

A plan only at an event

The craft follows a coastline. It calculates a new plan only when the tracking error goes past a threshold, or when the horizon ends. Each tick on the rail is one plan, and the dashed line is the plan ahead. Make the horizon longer, and the craft plans less often.

From the paper An Event-Triggered Visual Servoing Predictive Control Strategy for the Surveillance of Contour-Based Areas using Multirotor Aerial Vehicles and the thesis Coastline Tracking for UAVs.
